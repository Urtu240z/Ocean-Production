# Hull-wide fresh foam, tail visibility and stern mist — 2026-10-18

## Result

Fresh Wake now refreshes pigment beside the center and forward wet hull as well as the stern. Its leading transition is feathered inside the existing swept contact footprint. Speed Whitening still provides progressive speed response, and Settling S still restores the older filament pattern. No history format, dispatch count or memory allocation was added for this extension. Filament Deformation and Filament Speed are not required.

Tail Jet retains its authored anchor, interpolation, bank response, Splash 3/4 selection and residual-airborne behavior. Its transparent material already has render priority 100; the evidence did not justify raising priority or removing hull occlusion. It also clips against the published stern waterline. A frozen real-ocean pose placed the nozzle at -0.586 m with water at +0.036 m: most of the root was genuinely submerged. Brightness and PNG tint cannot recover discarded pixels.

BowSprayRoot → Tail Jet → Visibility now exposes Surface Fade Depth M (0.25 m) and Water Core Strength (0.45). The first fades shallow aerated water gradually. The second strengthens the masked water film and reduces washed-out wet highlights against white wake. Setting each to zero recovers the previous respective behavior. Full submersion, sprite coverage, authored opacity and solid hull occlusion remain in force. A low/downward nozzle can still require an artist adjustment to Sheet Height or Downward Curve; this change does not relocate the nozzle to the water surface.

BowSprayRoot → Stern Mist → Appearance → Settings adds an independent local GPU emitter: 28 particles, 0.42 s life, 30 Hz simulation with interpolation, turbulent movement and internally warped soft billboards. Color, Opacity, Amount, Offset, Width, Length, Height, Lifetime, Turbulence, speed thresholds and editor preview are adjustable with Spanish help text. The cloud grows with throttle/speed while wet, fades when coasting, and hides on airborne/dry contact. Spatial fade limits it to the stern. X/Z follow the nozzle; Y is measured above the higher of the sampled water or nozzle. No new water query, ocean mesh, FFT, per-pixel boat loop or CPU particle loop is involved.

## Isolated effect cost

D3D12, 1280×720, vsync/frame cap disabled, identical camera and white-water backdrop. ABBA order: previous tail/no mist, revised tail + mist, revised, previous. Each state warms for 80 frames, then records 1.2 s. CPU visual timing includes the existing spray controller and new gating; GPU timing includes particle simulation and draw. A disabled mist helper remains in the baseline, so this is an effect on/off comparison in the current implementation.

| Boats | GPU off/on ms | Render CPU off/on ms | Visual update CPU off/on ms | Real frame off/on ms | Static RAM growth during four intervals |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.227 / 0.241 | 0.058 / 0.061 | 0.056 / 0.056 | 0.602 / 0.543 | 1,924 B |
| 6 | 0.232 / 0.255 | 0.056 / 0.069 | 0.164 / 0.173 | 0.634 / 0.626 | 1,316 B |

Measured added GPU time is approximately 0.014 ms for one boat and 0.024 ms for six. Short real-frame timings include substantial scheduling/warm-up variation; their reduction is not a speedup claim. The allocation budget is 28/168 particles, not a growing trail. Static RAM growth excludes initial effect allocation and is only a short stability check, not a long leak test or total GPU-memory measurement. These numbers isolate the VFX and must not be presented as complete racing-scene performance.
